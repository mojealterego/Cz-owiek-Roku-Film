# CZŁOWIEK ROKU — STORY BIBLE

## STATUS
SOURCE-DERIVED / ADAPTATION CONTROL DOCUMENT

This document consolidates the narrative spine of the 136-page screenplay without replacing the screenplay itself.

## 1. LOGLINE

Andrzej, a 42-year-old miner and accomplished photographer, loses the stable life he believed he had when his relationship with Weronika collapses and access to his daughter Zuzia becomes the center of an escalating legal and institutional conflict. What begins as a private family catastrophe becomes a struggle against courts, police, bureaucracy, professional networks and public narratives. After physical and psychological collapse, Andrzej turns his camera, writing and public voice into instruments of resistance and becomes part of a wider movement of fathers.

## 2. NARRATIVE ENGINE

The screenplay runs on three interlocking conflicts:

1. **PRIVATE / FAMILY** — Andrzej vs. the collapse of his relationship with Weronika and the loss of ordinary contact with Zuzia.
2. **INSTITUTIONAL / LEGAL** — Andrzej vs. procedures, allegations, orders, court proceedings, opinions and administrative machinery.
3. **PUBLIC / SOCIAL** — Andrzej transforms private pain into photography, writing, media appearances and the "Strajk Ojców" movement.

The three lines progressively merge. The system first appears as an abstract wall and later acquires concrete faces, names and relationships.

## 3. ACT I — PĘKNIĘCIE
**0:00–21:00**

The film opens in December 2023 at the Adam Mickiewicz Theatre in Cieszyn during the "Człowiek Roku" ceremony. Andrzej is publicly successful: respected, decorated, known as an artist and local figure. The opening contrasts the polished public ritual with the narrator's understanding of photography as an attempt to capture truth hidden in shadow.

The apparent success is immediately undermined by the private story. The family relationship deteriorates. The conflict with Weronika escalates. Access to Zuzia becomes restricted and the machinery of allegations and protective measures begins to shape the father's reality.

The emotional rupture becomes concrete when Andrzej sees Zuzia at a distance and cannot simply approach her. The child who previously ran toward him now reacts with fear. The physical distance of the street becomes the visual representation of the institutional distance imposed between father and daughter.

Act I establishes the central paradox: the man publicly celebrated as "Człowiek Roku" is simultaneously being constructed, inside another narrative, as a dangerous person.

## 4. ACT II — WALKA Z MUREM
**21:00–50:30**

The conflict enters police, medical, legal and social institutions. Andrzej attempts to respond through documentation, lawyers, evidence and persistence. The screenplay repeatedly contrasts the language of lived experience with the language of forms, files and procedures.

The pressure becomes physical. At the Foundation Anielski Młyn, Andrzej suffers a severe collapse and is found unconscious by Artur. He is taken to intensive care. The hospital sequence converts the institutional environment into a visual continuation of the legal machinery: white walls, antiseptic smell, monitor beeps and professional distance.

After surviving the collapse, Andrzej continues the fight. His professional identity as a photographer becomes an important counterweight to the institutional narrative. He is still capable of creating images even when his personal world is disintegrating.

The screenplay then turns photography into action. The "Projekt W." sequence becomes a major reversal: instead of producing the expected portraits of Weronika, Andrzej builds a new exhibition around Wiktoria. Artur, Michał and Julka help install the work. The public expects scandal or reconciliation; the exhibition redirects the gaze.

## 5. ACT II — PROJECT W. AS COUNTERATTACK

The night before the exhibition, Andrzej and the team install large black-and-white portraits of Wiktoria at EkoTradycja. Posters appear around Cieszyn and social media speculation grows around the mysterious "Projekt W."

On 2 May 2024, the exhibition opens on Weronika's 40th birthday. The screenplay deliberately places the private conflict inside a public cultural event. Weronika objects to the publication of her image. Andrzej responds from a position of controlled irony. The expected spectacle becomes a different story.

The exhibition is also a statement about the artist reclaiming control over his own image and work. The camera that once documented the breakdown of his family becomes a tool for choosing what story will be shown to the public.

## 6. ACT III — BEZ PRAWA
**50:30–1:22:09**

The legal conflict becomes the dominant reality. Courtrooms become increasingly claustrophobic, bureaucratic and physically oppressive. Andrzej is repeatedly confronted with the difference between what happened in lived reality and what exists in the official record.

The family court sequence places Andrzej opposite Weronika and Wojciech. The language of "dobro dziecka" becomes a recurring institutional formula. Andrzej's internal narration treats the courtroom as a machine that processes people into documents.

The conflict expands beyond one relationship. Karol, Andrzej's neighbour, becomes an important source of information and later assists with documents and court-related writing. Other people around Andrzej become witnesses, helpers, institutional actors or parts of the social network surrounding the dispute.

A major narrative shift occurs when the anonymous system becomes identifiable. Connections between people, institutions and prior relationships begin to look like a closed network. The screenplay deliberately moves from personal tragedy toward an investigative/conspiracy-thriller mechanism without abandoning the grounded social drama.

## 7. STRAJK OJCÓW / PUBLIC MOVEMENT

Andrzej encounters other fathers with similar experiences. Their stories form a collective chorus rather than a single isolated grievance. The movement becomes the social extension of his personal story.

The street protest is framed as a gathering of men who have lost ordinary access to their children and feel invisible inside institutional procedures. The screenplay emphasizes repetition: different names, similar mechanisms, similar allegations, similar orders and similar feelings of exclusion.

After the protest, media outlets approach Andrzej. The television sequence is deliberately cynical: the private pain is converted into a media product. Studio lighting, makeup, cameras and presenters create another institutional environment. Andrzej initially believes speaking publicly might change something, then realizes that the media also consumes the story as a commodity.

## 8. ATTEMPTED RECONCILIATION

After the protest and media cycle, Andrzej attempts one more time to negotiate. The screenplay frames this as a dangerous return to hope: he wants to bury the hatchet and believe that an agreement is possible.

The attempt fails. The conflict returns to the legal machine. The emotional mechanism is important: Andrzej is not simply becoming harder; he repeatedly risks trusting the possibility of resolution and is repeatedly forced back into confrontation.

## 9. FINAL LEGAL COLLAPSE

The later court proceedings become increasingly fatalistic. Evidence, expert opinions, statements and previous words are reinterpreted inside the institutional narrative.

A crucial late-stage phone call confirms that an adverse decision has been maintained and that statements from Andrzej's own public writing have been used against him. His language becomes evidence against him. The screenplay explicitly frames the process as the system validating its own previous conclusions.

The narrative reaches the point where Andrzej is not merely fighting a person or a single decision. He is trapped inside a self-reinforcing institutional story.

## 10. ACT IV — OPINIA (I FINAŁ)
**1:22:09–1:32:39**

After more than a year in the foundation, Andrzej rents a new apartment in autumn 2025. It is supposed to represent a new beginning but initially functions as an empty shell.

He prepares a children's room for Zuzia even though she is absent. The room becomes a physical monument to an absent child: new furniture, butterfly bedding and an empty space waiting for someone who does not arrive.

The screenplay then moves into Andrzej's final psychological breakdown. He cannot eat or sleep. The sterile apartment becomes a visual continuation of the institutional spaces that have dominated the story.

The final sequence is not a conventional victory. Andrzej has lost the legal battle and suffered profound personal damage, but he retains the ability to write and to record what happened.

## 11. FINAL IMAGE / LAST SOUND

Andrzej finishes a letter and places it beside a photograph of Zuzia.

Absolute silence.

A camera shutter sounds.

Cut to black.

The shutter is simultaneously:
- the photographer's signature;
- the sound of memory;
- the final recorded fragment of his relationship with Zuzia;
- a refusal to let the story disappear completely.

## 12. CHARACTER ARC — ANDRZEJ

**STATE 1 — CONTROL:** successful artist, father, husband, publicly recognized man.

**STATE 2 — RUPTURE:** relationship collapse and restriction of contact with Zuzia.

**STATE 3 — RESISTANCE:** documentation, lawyers, evidence, institutions, photography.

**STATE 4 — COLLAPSE:** physical breakdown, hospital, exhaustion.

**STATE 5 — COUNTER-NARRATIVE:** photography and "Projekt W." reclaim authorship of the public story.

**STATE 6 — COLLECTIVE:** other fathers appear; personal pain becomes social movement.

**STATE 7 — PUBLIC EXPOSURE:** media turns suffering into a consumable story.

**STATE 8 — LAST HOPE:** attempted reconciliation.

**STATE 9 — FINAL DEFEAT:** legal machinery confirms its own narrative.

**STATE 10 — WITNESS:** Andrzej writes, records and leaves a final document. He does not receive a conventional triumph; he retains authorship of his testimony.

## 13. DRAMATIC PARADOX

The title "Człowiek Roku" becomes progressively ironic.

At the beginning it means public recognition.

By the middle it becomes a contradiction: the celebrated man is simultaneously treated as a problem by institutions.

By the end it becomes a question: what remains of a public identity when private and institutional narratives have destroyed the person underneath it?
